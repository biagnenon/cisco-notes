## Notes

### Network Types

The ***Internet*** is not owned by any individual or group, it is a worldwide collection of interconnected networks, exchanging information using common standards.

The different type of networks are :
- ***Small Home Networks*** | connect few computers to each other and to the internet.
- ***Small Office and Home Office Networks (SOHO)*** | connect home office or remote office to corporate networks and allows access to centralized resources.
- ***Medium to Large Networks*** | used by corporations and schools, can have many locations with many interconnected hosts.
- ***World Wide Network*** | the internet is a network of networks connecting hundreds of millions of devices.

***Radio frequency Identification (RFID)*** tags that can be placed in or on objects to track them or monitor sensors.

### Data Transmission

***Data*** is information that can be stored, transmitted, or manipulated.

The following categories are used to classify types of personal data :
	- ***Volunteered data*** | created and explicitly shared by individuals.
	- ***Observed data*** | collected by watching, measuring or recording individuals' activities.
	- ***Inferred data*** | data generated by the analysis of volunteered or observed data.

A ***bit*** is short for ***binary digit***. Each bit can have one of two possible values, 0 or 1, and represents the smallest unit of data. Computers and networks store and transmit data as a series of bits. A ***byte*** is group of eight bits.

Data must be converted into signals transmissible over the network media. ***Media*** refers to the physical medium on which a signal is transmitted.

Three common method of signal transmission :
	- ***Electrical signals |*** copper wire (electrical pulses)
	- ***Optical signals |*** optic fiber (light pulses)
	- ***Wireless signals |*** infrared, microwaves or radio waves

### Bandwidth and Throughput

***Bandwidth*** is the capacity of a medium to carry data. Digital bandwidth measures the amount of data that can flow through the network in a given time frame. it is measured in the number of bits that can theoretically be sent across the media in second.

***Throughput*** is the actual rate at which data is successfully transmitted over the network in a given time frame. It does not usually match the given bandwidth. In a network with multiple segments, throughput can not be faster than the slower link of the path from the sending device to the receiving device. Many factors influence throughput.

***Latency*** refers to the amount of time, including delays, for data to be transmitted over the network.

## Real-World Connection

- In a home network, the bandwidth is the theoretical maximum, but actual throughput is often lower because of several factors like distance or interference, or the slowest link in the path. Latency matters for activities such as video calls or gaming.

## Gotchas / Exam Traps

- ***Bandwidth ≠ Throughput*** | Bandwidth is theoretical capacity while throughput is actual successful transmission rate.
- ***Internet ≠ World Wide Web*** | The Web is a service that runs on the internet
